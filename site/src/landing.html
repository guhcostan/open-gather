An open source virtual office for remote teams: walk around a 2D map, bump into teammates and talk with proximity audio and video. Built with Go, PixiJS and LiveKit, and designed from day one to be cheap to host yourself.

Everything a virtual office needs

A complete loop from avatar to conversation, with the expensive parts handled by the right tool.

Walk and meet

Keyboard movement with collisions, a following camera and smooth remote avatars through prediction and interpolation.

Get around fast

Run with Shift or toggle it with R. Double-click any spot, or a point on the minimap, to run there along a path the server finds. "Walk to" anyone from the people panel.

Pets

Pick a cat, dog, bunny, fox, chick, slime, owl or axolotl that trots after you. Drawn in code and animated on each screen: zero bandwidth.

Presence

Wave at anyone in the office (they can run straight to you), raise your hand, set a status note, dance. Busy means do not disturb.

Proximity conversations

Audio and video start when you get close and stop when you leave, with hysteresis so nothing flaps at the edge.

Consent and status

Nothing is captured before you opt in. Busy, away and invisible people are never pulled into a call.

Meeting rooms

Areas with explicit access: open, members only, admins only or an allow list, enforced on the server. Lock a room; outsiders knock.

Screen sharing

Up to 1080p for readable text, with cheap low layers for small tiles. Expand it over the map, go full screen or picture-in-picture.

Spotlight and announcements

Step on the stage to broadcast to the whole office. Administrators can send a banner to everyone online.

Whiteboards, notes and portals

Collaborative boards that persist, notes, embedded sites and images, portals across the office, and an editor to build your own map.

Chat and minimap

Office, conversation and direct messages. A minimap with live head counts per area shows where people are.

Phones and tablets

An on-screen movement pad on touch screens, and optional sounds and desktop notifications for waves, knocks and messages.

Self-hosted

One Go binary, SQLite in WAL mode and a self-hosted LiveKit. No paid service is required to run the whole product.

Light by design

Three concerns, kept apart: the world, the data and the media.

Server

One goroutine owns each office. A spatial grid and areas of interest mean a player is only compared with people nearby. Updates are batched, incremental and coalesced for slow clients.

Browser

The static map is one texture. Only what the camera sees is drawn, hidden tabs stop rendering but keep calls alive, and an economy mode caps the frame rate.

Media

A real SFU, never a P2P mesh. Rooms are small, tokens are scoped to one room, and access is revoked on the server when you leave.

Conversations that behave

Proximity chat is easy to get wrong. The rules are explicit and tested.

  • Hysteresis The distance to leave is larger than the one to join, and both wait a short time.
  • No hopping Standing between two conversations keeps you in yours. To switch, you leave first.
  • No chains A line of avatars cannot merge the whole office into one call.
  • Rooms are isolated Someone in the corridor is not pulled into a meeting room's call.

Honest status

Alpha software. Here is what has actually been checked, and what has not.

Verified

  • Go tests for the world, store, admin API and media tokens, also with the race detector
  • A real-browser suite (Chrome + a real LiveKit) on every push to main and every pull request: proximity calls with real audio and video RTP, private rooms and offices, screen sharing, invites, admin, editor, attacks on tokens, reconnection, accessibility (axe-core), and every social feature
  • The public demo on a free Oracle VM: real audio and video over UDP across the internet

Measured once, not a capacity claim

  • On the free 1 GB demo VM, loaded over the internet: 100 bots smooth, 500 saturated; a 4-person video call and 20 people in audio groups clean
  • On a laptop with the generator on the same machine: up to 1,000 bots without media and 40 people in calls

Not yet

  • TURN through UDP-blocked networks
  • Load on a larger reference server, with repeated runs
  • A two-hour soak
  • Screen reader testing by people
  • Verified prices

Self-host it

Try the whole stack on your machine with Docker. The first person to join becomes the administrator.

git clone {{REPO}}.git
cd open-gather
docker compose -f deploy/docker-compose.local.yml up --build
# open http://localhost:8080 in two browser profiles

For a real server, the production Compose file adds automatic HTTPS (Caddy), a LiveKit media server and invite-only joins, using the prebuilt image for amd64 and arm64. To hack on the code, ./scripts/dev.sh starts LiveKit, the Go server and Vite.
