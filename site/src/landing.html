Everything a virtual office needs

A complete loop from avatar to conversation, with the expensive parts handled by the right tool.

Walk and meet

Keyboard movement with collisions, a following camera and smooth remote avatars through prediction and interpolation.

Proximity conversations

Audio and video start when you get close and stop when you leave, with hysteresis so nothing flaps at the edge.

Consent and status

Nothing is captured before you opt in. Busy, away and invisible people are never pulled into a call.

Meeting rooms

Areas with explicit access: open, members only, admins only or an allow list, enforced on the server.

Chat and screen sharing

Office chat, conversation chat and direct messages, plus screen sharing inside a live conversation.

Self-hosted

One Go binary, SQLite in WAL mode and a self-hosted LiveKit. No paid service is required to run the whole product.

Light by design

Three concerns, kept apart: the world, the data and the media.

Server

One goroutine owns each office. A spatial grid and areas of interest mean a player is only compared with people nearby. Updates are batched, incremental and coalesced for slow clients.

Browser

The static map is one texture. Only what the camera sees is drawn, hidden tabs stop rendering but keep calls alive, and an economy mode caps the frame rate.

Media

A real SFU, never a P2P mesh. Rooms are small, tokens are scoped to one room, and access is revoked on the server when you leave.

Conversations that behave

Proximity chat is easy to get wrong. The rules are explicit and tested.

  • Hysteresis The distance to leave is larger than the one to join, and both wait a short time.
  • No hopping Standing between two conversations keeps you in yours. To switch, you leave first.
  • No chains A line of avatars cannot merge the whole office into one call.
  • Rooms are isolated Someone in the corridor is not pulled into a meeting room's call.
